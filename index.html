Capture, process and route audio on your desktop

Reverblabr takes the sound of an application, a microphone or everything your computer plays, sends it through effects you put together block by block, and plays it, records it or hands it to other programs as a microphone of its own.

Download Reverblabr 1.0.2 See every feature

For Linux, on KDE Plasma 6 with PipeWire, in English and fourteen other languages. Free software under the Mozilla Public License 2.0.

How a session works

You build a session on a canvas. Sound comes in at its sources, travels along wires through the blocks you add, and leaves at its outputs. The wires turn orange while sound goes through them, and anything can be changed while it plays.

Sources

The sound of one application, a microphone or another input, or everything the computer plays. Capturing an application doesn't move it: it goes on playing where it was, and nothing is installed into it.

Blocks in between

More than twenty built-in effects, from volume, equalisers and compressors to noise and hum removal, reverb and pitch shift. Meters at any point in a lane. Blocks that shape the sound for each ear. And plugins in the LV2, LADSPA, CLAP and VST3 formats; which formats, and which plugins, depends on the package.

Outputs

Any speakers or headphones, a recording, or a virtual microphone for other programs. A lane can also go to an internet radio server, or to a video platform with a picture made for it.

What you can do with it

Make a call or a stream sound better

Take the noise out of a voice, even out its level and shape its tone. Play the result into a virtual microphone and pick that in your call or streaming program, so everyone hears the cleaned-up voice.

The blocks for voices

Send sound between programs

Make virtual outputs and microphones. Other programs see them as ordinary sound cards, so you can run your browser or music player through a session before you hear it, or hand your processed voice to a call. They stay until you delete them, whether Reverblabr is running or not.

Virtual devices

Record what you hear

Record one application, a microphone, or both side by side, to WAV, FLAC, MP3, Opus and more. A crash doesn't cost you the recording. The Files tab lists everything a session recorded, to play back, rename and tag.

Recording

Shape the sound for your ears

The Hearing blocks lift quiet sound band by band, for each ear on its own, and can be set up from an audiogram. Others move high or low sounds into a range that is easier to hear. Every band stays under limits you set. Reverblabr is not a medical device and doesn't replace a hearing aid.

Hearing

Change it while it plays

Add a block to a running session and it connects itself. Open its card and change it while you listen: the sound follows without a click, and Undo takes any change back.

The window

What it does differently

  • It leaves your applications alone

    Capturing an application only adds a link. The application keeps playing where it was, and when Reverblabr stops, or even crashes, the link is gone.

  • A plugin can't take the session down

    Every plugin runs in a process of its own. If one crashes or hangs, its block is bypassed and the sound goes on, and Restart brings it back with its settings.

  • Sessions run without the window

    A small background engine plays them with the window closed, and through a crash of the window. Start them at login or on a timer, or from the tray, a panel widget, KRunner or a shortcut.

  • Real-time rules, checked by tools

    Nothing on the audio thread allocates memory or takes a lock, and the compiler and a real-time sanitizer check that, not only a review. A session too heavy for the computer goes quiet instead of taking the program down.

  • Private

    No usage statistics, no update checks, no network use of its own. Only the Broadcast and Live Stream blocks send anything, to the server you name, and their password or stream key stays in your desktop's password store.

  • At home on Plasma and PipeWire

    It talks to PipeWire directly and leaves WirePlumber in charge. The tray icon, notifications, global shortcuts, KRunner, the panel widget and KWallet are the desktop's own.

  • Scriptable, and it stays that way

    A D-Bus API does everything the window does, and reverblabr-ctl runs and changes sessions from a terminal or a script. Both are only ever added to, so a script keeps working.

  • Works without a mouse

    Every step works from the keyboard and with a screen reader, and a session can be shown as a list. In its own light and dark looks, text and every mark that means something meet the contrast of WCAG 2.1 AA.

  • Free, and open to any plugin

    The code is under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, chosen so that commercial plugins can be used as well as free ones.

How it started

In 2023 I gave up Apple products and moved to Linux. I was tired of being told what to do and how to use my own device. The move went well, but I missed a program for managing audio streams and putting filters on them, and nothing on Linux did what I needed. So I started writing my own, by hand, and spent much of that time learning the KDE API.

About eight months later the first version ran in a terminal and could do very little, and it took most of another year to make it more or less stable. In 2025 it got its first design and became a real KDE application. A few people I know started using it and asking for more, and I learned QML to give it a look of its own.

In the middle of 2026 someone I know told me he had partly lost his hearing through an illness, had to wear a hearing aid all day, and found nothing on Linux to help him. Together we gathered what was needed, and I built the blocks in the Hearing group.

By then the program I had written for myself was one that other people need too, so I'm releasing it for everyone. I first wanted a GNU licence, but it would have kept commercial plugins out, and I want any plugin to work with Reverblabr. So I chose the Mozilla Public License 2.0. I'd be happy if you find it useful.
